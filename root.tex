\begin{abstract}
Nearly every autonomous or semi-autonomous flipper controller assumes access
to an \emph{elevation map} of the ground under and in front of the robot. The terrain beneath the chassis is occluded from the sensors, so this map is remembered through odometry
integration, whose drift corrupts it. We present
\emph{Stabilizing Anti-Stuck} (\SAS),
a map-less controller that combines a stabilization term for body roll
and pitch with an anti-stuck term that frees the robot when it is stuck. \SAS{} uses as
input the IMU, the flipper joint angles, and a motion cue that only
tells whether the robot is moving; the cue is computed over a short window and never
accumulated, so \SAS{} does not inherit the odometry-integration drift that corrupts the
map. We deploy \SAS{} on a tracked robot with four flippers and evaluate it against manual
teleoperation and prior flipper controllers in a large comparison run, both in Gazebo
and in a real obstacle arena. In simulation and on the real robot, \SAS{} reaches traversal quality comparable to the
map-based controllers. In an additional experiment, a log hidden in tall grass was invisible to the LiDAR and
therefore absent from the map; the best map-based policy read the gap in the grass as a
drop and got stuck under the log, while \SAS{} crossed it in its default posture.
\end{abstract}
