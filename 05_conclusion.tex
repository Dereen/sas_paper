% ============================ CONCLUSION ============================
State-of-the-art flipper controllers share a precondition that is easy to overlook
in simulation and costly on a real robot: they need an elevation map of the ground
under and around the chassis. That terrain is
occluded from onboard sensing, so the map can only be carried beneath a moving robot
by integrating odometry, a dead-reckoning process whose drift corrupts it precisely
on the loose, step-like terrain where flippers are required.

We took the opposite stance and asked how far a flipper controller can go without a
map and without localization. \SAS{} is mapless, localization-free, and
integration-free: it regulates body attitude directly from the gravity-referenced,
drift-free roll and pitch of the IMU, and recovers from being stuck from a
short-horizon motion cue, superimposing the two under a directional-priority blend
that keeps stabilization in control. It is not a universal replacement (with no
terrain preview its peak traversal quality trails a well-fed map-based controller,
and its reactive recovery can still oscillate), but it keeps the robot level and
remains operational in exactly the regime where the onboard map, and therefore every
map-based baseline, breaks down.

Future work targets a more robust motion cue by fusing the LiDAR-ICP odometry with
camera odometry, which should hold up in the occluded, feature-poor, or flat places
where ICP alone struggles; and a fallback scheme that defers to a map-based controller
when the onboard map is trustworthy and to \SAS{} when it is not.
